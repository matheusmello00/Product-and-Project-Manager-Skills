# Interview craft — User Interview Prep reference

Index:

2. Hypotheses

## 1. Research goal

- One sentence, specific, learnable. Starts with "Understand…" or "Discover…".
- Never "Validate…" or "Confirm…": they bias the whole study toward a yes.
- If the input is a solution ("Will users like the swap feature?"), ask what problem it assumes and write the goal about that problem ("Understand how students currently exchange textbooks and where it breaks down").
- Scope to one behaviour or situation. Two goals mean two studies.

## 2. Hypotheses

- 3–5 beliefs the PM is likely carrying, phrased as claims about users' current behaviour or pain.
- Each has **true if we hear…** (observable evidence) and **false if we hear…** (observable counter-evidence). A hypothesis without a falsifier is an opinion.
- Weak or untestable (e.g. "users want a better experience"): say so, then propose a testable version.
- If context was provided (feedback, analytics, personas), cite the source of each hypothesis; otherwise label it "assumption".

## 3. Interview flow (30 minutes)

| Block | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 5 min | rapport, role and context, set expectations (no right answers, not selling) |
| Core exploration | 20 min | the last concrete time they faced the problem; what they did, tried, paid, felt; workarounds |
| Wrap-up | 5 min | anything missed, referrals, permission to follow up |

Core questions go from **concrete story → details → impact**. Keep 2–4 core questions; depth beats breadth. For other lengths, scale the blocks proportionally.

## 4. Writing questions (Mom Test)

Rules of thumb:
- Ask about **their life and past**, not your idea or a hypothetical future.
- Ask for **specific recent episodes** ("Tell me about the last time…"), not general habits ("Do you usually…").
- Prefer "what / how / when / who / tell me about"; avoid yes/no.
- Never open with "Would you…" or "Do you think…".
- No compliments-bait, no pitching, no mention of the PM's solution.
- Maximum **12 questions** in the whole kit.

Check each question:

| Check | Fails when… | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Past, not future | "Would you use…?" | "When did you last have to…?" |
| Their life, not my idea | names the product or feature | describe the situation instead |
| Open | yes/no answer | start with what/how/tell me |
| Single | two questions in one | split or drop one |
| Neutral | hints the right answer ("Don't you find it slow?") | "How did that go?" |

## 5. Follow-up prompts and bias list

Every core question gets **at least 2** follow-ups, chosen from:
- "Tell me more about that."
- "What happened next?"
- "Why was that important?"
- "What did you try before?"
- "How did you feel at that point?"
- "Can you show me how you do it?"
- "Who else was involved?"

Interviewer biases to design against: **leading questions**, **confirmation bias** (hearing what supports the hypothesis), **hypothetical bias** (opinions about what they would do), **politeness bias** (friendly answers about your idea), **solution pitching**, **filling silence**, **stacking questions**.

## 6. Things to watch for

Beyond words: hesitation before an answer, workarounds (spreadsheets, sticky notes, copy-paste), emotional signals (sighs, laughter, frustration), what they do versus what they say, tools they open to show you, things they skip or minimise, repeated mentions of the same pain.

## 7. Recruiting screener note

Keep it to 3–4 lines in the brief: the **segment**, the **must-have recent behaviour** (e.g. "bought or sold a used textbook in the last 3 months"), **exclusions** (e.g. colleagues, people who work on the product), and a suggested **sample size** (5–8 per segment as a starting point; stop when answers repeat). Do not invent recruiting channels or incentives.

## 8. Consent and recording note

State in the brief: explain the purpose in one sentence, **ask permission to record** before starting, say that answers are confidential and used only for the research, and that the participant can skip any question or stop. Never assume consent; if recording is declined, take notes only.
